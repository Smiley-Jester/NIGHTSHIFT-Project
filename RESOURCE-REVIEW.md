## Quality and safety notes

- The first lesson has a compact Linux course plus a separate Windows Fundamentals walkthrough, so both operating systems are covered.
- Python links directly to the Python-for-hackers chapter of a 2 h 37 min freeCodeCamp course. The 33-minute chapter covers core Python concepts, `socket`, `requests` and a small script in a local CTF lab. It is not a full Python programming course by itself; the DFIRScience companion gives more beginner practice with files and data.
- OSINT and OpSec are separate named videos, not search links. Class Central lists the John Hammond OSINT course at 5/5, but that rating is based on one review only, so it should not be treated as statistically robust.
- The Metasploit selection favors Rapid7’s official overview and a project-lead interview over broad, unfiltered tutorials. This is introductory context, not a complete hands-on framework course.
- The Nessus companion covers scan interpretation and remediation, but its original lab setup contains dated security-setting choices. The site warns not to reproduce those settings on an everyday device.
- The capstone primary video has nearly one million views and covers the five-phase process; the O’Reilly companion is a structured beginner course with quizzes and a sandbox but may require a subscription.
- View counts and ratings change. Popularity is a signal, not proof of quality; relevance, instructor credibility, clarity and alignment with the lesson matter more.
- All hands-on practice is limited to provider-supplied labs, learner-owned systems or assets explicitly covered by written authorization and scope. Never scan or access public or third-party systems without explicit written permission.
